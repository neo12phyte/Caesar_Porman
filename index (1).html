Cultural Frames and Teaching

About Me

Living and working in Taiwan has shown me how classroom norms and family expectations shape student behavior: many students stay quiet out of respect for the teacher, and success is often measured only by grades.

I see silence as a culturally shaped response, not disengagement — and that changes how I design lessons and assess learning. It means building in more ways for students to show me what they understand, on their own terms.

Silence isn’t disengagement. It’s a signal to design differently.

Classroom Practices

How I Teach

I lower social and academic risk by pre-teaching and scaffolding, and I use predictable routines so every student knows how to participate.

Scaffolding
Pre-teaching & guided structureShort readings, guided notes, and reflection prompts that lower the barrier to entry before a discussion or task begins.
Routines
Predictable participation structuresThink–pair–share, jigsaw, and anonymous question boxes give every student a low-risk way into the conversation.
Assessment
Multiple ways to show learningWritten responses, recorded explanations, projects, and portfolios — so students with evening cram-school commitments or different strengths can still demonstrate mastery.
Discourse
Civil discourse, co-createdStudents help set the norms for discussion, and structured protocols keep conversations about equity and rights balanced.
